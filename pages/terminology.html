Transportation Problem Terminologies

Important terms you need to understand before solving problems.

Source

The location where goods originate or are supplied.

Example: Factory D.

Destination

The location where goods must be delivered.

Example: Warehouse A.

Supply

The quantity of goods available at each source.

Demand

The quantity of goods required by each destination.

Transportation Cost

The cost of transporting one unit from a particular source to a destination.

Allocation

The quantity assigned to a specific transportation route.

Decision Variable

The unknown quantity shipped from source i to destination j, represented by xᵢⱼ.

Objective Function

The mathematical expression that represents the total cost or profit to optimize.

Constraints

The mathematical restrictions imposed by supply, demand, and route availability.

Feasible Solution

A solution that satisfies all supply and demand constraints.

Basic Feasible Solution

A feasible allocation represented by an independent basis of occupied cells.

Optimal Solution

A feasible solution that produces the minimum cost or maximum profit.

Dummy Source

An artificial source added when total demand exceeds total supply.

Dummy Destination

An artificial destination added when total supply exceeds total demand.

Penalty

The difference between the two smallest available costs in a row or column when using VAM.

Opportunity Cost

The estimated change in total cost when introducing a previously unused route.

Degeneracy

A condition where the number of independent basic cells is fewer than m+n−1.

Closed Loop

An alternating horizontal and vertical path used to adjust transportation allocations.

Entering Cell

An unoccupied cell selected to enter the current transportation basis.

Leaving Cell

A basic cell that leaves the basis after an allocation adjustment.

Reduced Cost

The opportunity-cost indicator used in the MODI method.

Shadow Price

A dual value associated with a supply or demand constraint.
